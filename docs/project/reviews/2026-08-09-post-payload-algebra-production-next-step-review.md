## Recommendation

Build one compact multi-step algebra article through the frozen
`kp.article.v1` contract and an existing canonical algebra animation. Treat
the current reader projection as a competent production workbench rather than
the final layout. During this caller, change layout or grammar only for a
demonstrated blocker.

The article should pressure:

- ordinary explanatory prose alongside short attention cues;
- more than one semantic motion block over persistent algebraic objects;
- inline and display KaTeX, semantic `kp-ref:` links, and direct checkpoints;
- searchable static publication and no-JavaScript reading;
- whole-file CodeMirror/Vim editing, invalid-draft recovery, and no-reload save;
- direct seek, rewind, and reconstruction without replaying intermediate
  animation;
- the 124,778-byte common reader closure as the pre-caller payload baseline.

Measure the production leverage explicitly: authoring time, reused versus
bespoke code, new shared infrastructure, payload delta, layout exceptions,
and defects found in the authoring loop. The mandatory human checkpoint should
judge the explanatory argument, attention handoff, and authoring experience;
it should not require selecting KP's final layout.

After review, extract only a seam demonstrated by both economics and algebra.
If the second caller is cheaper and the teaching result is credible, build one
adjacent third lesson before reopening layout selection. Use those real
articles to compare split, stacked, or deck projections from the same source,
rather than attempting to settle layout from an isolated economics specimen.

## Preservation Boundary

Preserve the frozen article grammar, framework-neutral semantic animation and
clock, searchable static output, build-time KaTeX/MathML, direct navigation,
the learner payload ceilings, and the current economics source. Do not resume
linear algebra, broad renderer promotion, final navigation geometry, or public
product work inside this article slice.

## Expected Decision Gate

At the end of the algebra caller, answer three questions:

1. Was a second serious article materially cheaper than the first?
2. Did the shared semantic objects and motion blocks improve the explanation,
   rather than merely decorate it?
3. Which repeated friction is now evidenced strongly enough to justify one
   shared fix?

Positive answers justify a small three-lesson teaching sequence. Negative
answers identify a bounded authoring, runtime, or projection problem without
invalidating the broader semantic-animation thesis.
