## Checkpoint Questions

1. Can the learner always tell which semantic step is current, including on
   still beats with no equation motion?
2. During a timed rewrite, do Previous and Next remain responsive and settle
   exactly one requested adjacent beat without a delayed second advance?
3. Does the slider remain a direct semantic control rather than competing with
   passage scroll or snap correction?
4. In Safari, do equation cadence, KaTeX fit, and scroll ownership remain
   predictable through rapid forward and reverse use?
5. Does the Graph3D surface-to-contour card expose the important motion and
   labels without clipping or competing with its passage?
6. At phone width, does each card remain readable without making the learner
   manage the layout?
7. Across all four cards, does attention move coherently between prose,
   stage, and controls, or does one persistent region compete for focus?
8. In the TypeScript card, are the duplicated rules, helper fusion, and two
   call-site replacements legible without making the source feel too small or
   busy?

## Promotion Boundary

Approval would select the present four-card shell as the reference for one
observed educator or learner session. It would not authorize another pressure
caller, automatic deck generation, a shared embedding package, catalogue-wide
migration, or a universal semantic model. A failed checkpoint should produce
a named, observable defect and the smallest card-local or shared-shell repair
that addresses it.
