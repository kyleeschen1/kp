# Animation Catalogue Backlog Next-Step Review

Date: 2026-08-14
Status: current selection review; no implementation authorized

## Outcome

KP has 40 loadable, lazy catalogue assets: 24 equation surfaces, seven graph
surfaces, three diagram surfaces, one composite surface, and five programming
surfaces. That is a strong executable portfolio, but not 40 polished teaching
animations. Several rows are generated equation fixtures, diagnostic samples,
or narrow renderer proofs.

The remaining backlog is best understood as missing transformation laws, not
missing subject labels. Existing assets prove cancellation, distribution,
factoring, fraction simplification, exponent expansion, function wrapping,
inequality reversal, exact fraction quantity, place-value carrying, vector
projection, synchronized graph models, integral area sweep, code refactoring,
and recursive Scheme evaluation. The gaps below require new semantic and
motion content.

## Important Partial Implementations

- Generated matrix-vector and matrix-matrix equation fixtures do not supply a
  spatial 2 x 2 linear-map animation. The Pour checkpoint remains unapproved
  and tabled.
- Derivative-rule and tangent-graph assets do not supply the complete
  difference-quotient/secant-to-tangent bridge.
- Integral and FTC assets do not supply Riemann-sum refinement with persistent
  accumulation.
- The Jacobian/Hessian comparison is diagnostic and a retirement candidate;
  it does not supply local-linear-map or curvature pedagogy.
- The eigenvector product checkpoint is not a promoted catalogue animation.
- The Graph3D mesh-to-donut row proves bounded hosting, not a general 3D
  transformation library.
- The Lisp lambda row and Scheme factorial exemplar do not yet form a broad
  S-expression or SICP animation family.

## Missing Or Incomplete Catalogue Animations

### Near, high-leverage transformation exemplars

1. Place-value subtraction with dependency-aware borrowing.
2. Fraction repartition and equivalence while quantity remains invariant.
3. Function translation, reflection, stretch, and compression across equation
   and graph.
4. Geometric dissection and rearrangement with area and adjacency invariants.
5. Quadratic completion, explicit plus/minus branching, and graph-root
   correspondence.
6. Difference quotient and secant converging to derivative and tangent.
7. Riemann sums refining into a persistent accumulated area.
8. A novice-readable code wrap/unwrap transformation as the second code
   topology after extraction/refactoring.

### Arithmetic and algebra breadth

1. Common-denominator alignment and fraction addition/subtraction.
2. Denominator cancellation and numerator fission/fusion variants.
3. Mixed-number and improper-fraction representation changes.
4. Multi-digit multiplication through partial products.
5. Multi-digit division through iterative consumption and remainder.
6. Ratios, rates, percent, and unit-aware proportional scaling.
7. Rational-expression addition, simplification, and domain restrictions.
8. Polynomial factoring and division with persistent remainder identity.
9. Logarithm expansion/recombination and exponential/log inverse continuity.
10. Two-variable systems solved through elimination.

### Calculus and continuous change

1. Limits as coordinated symbolic, numeric, and graphical approach.
2. Taylor approximation and local linearization.
3. Gradient as directional change.
4. Jacobian as a local linear map rather than a label comparison.
5. Hessian as curvature or a quadratic form.
6. Optimization built from gradient, curvature, and constraint evidence.

### Linear algebra, still deliberately tabled

1. Vector addition and scalar multiplication as spatial operations.
2. Apply a 2 x 2 matrix to vectors and the plane.
3. Matrix multiplication as composed dot products and composed maps.
4. Elementary row operations with equation/system correspondence.
5. Determinant as oriented area or volume scale.
6. Inverse as undoing a map.
7. Basis and coordinate change with object identity preserved.
8. Eigenvectors, eigenvalues, and invariant subspaces as a canonical catalogue
   asset.
9. Diagonalization, followed much later by SVD.

### Geometry, trigonometry, probability, and networks

1. Unit-circle motion synchronized with the sine graph.
2. Pythagorean rearrangement and unit-aware scale drawings.
3. Geometric-series partial sums and limiting aggregation.
4. Conditional-probability trees with evidence-driven renormalization.
5. Linked data plots and summary statistics.
6. Breadth-first search synchronized across code, queue, and graph.

### Domain follow-ons already discussed but intentionally deferred

1. Consumer and producer surplus.
2. Taxes, controls, externalities, and deadweight loss through honest economics
   models rather than decorative shading.
3. Additional force, energy, and conservation transformations beyond the
   constant-force exemplar.
4. Meaningful 3D coordinate, rotation, projection, and surface
   transformations beyond the internal mesh-to-donut host proof.

## Recommendation

Choose one of three modes before implementation:

- **Learn most about the architecture:** place-value subtraction.
- **Add polished breadth fastest:** fraction equivalence and its short harvest.
- **Build the most immediately visual new artifact:** function-coordinate
  transformations.

In all modes, the next slice should build one independently reversible
catalogue exemplar, name its canonical reference and acceptance criteria, and
stop for visual review before any family extraction.
