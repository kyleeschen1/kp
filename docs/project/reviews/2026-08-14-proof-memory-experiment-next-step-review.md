# Proof-Memory Experiment Next-Step Review

Date: 2026-08-14
Status: accepted recommendation

## Question

Which first content slice best tests KP as a medium for seeing through and
retaining difficult symbolic arguments without reopening broad curriculum or
layout work?

## Candidate Review

Scores are relative within this decision: `5` is strongest, while risk uses
`5` for greatest speculative complexity.

| Candidate | Authoring | Reliability | Reuse | Slice | Risk | Recommendation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Normal matrices are unitarily diagonalizable | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 2 | Do next |
| Existing fraction-composition proof-memory projection | 5 | 5 | 3 | 5 | 1 | Useful baseline, but too elementary to validate the wedge |
| Singular value decomposition | 3 | 2 | 5 | 1 | 5 | Strong destination; too many representations and prerequisites for the first test |
| Gödel incompleteness proof | 2 | 2 | 5 | 1 | 5 | Ultimate symbol-identity case; defer until proof-memory authority is proven |
| Scheme factorial/SICP memory projection | 4 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 2 | Strong later contrast; code evaluation is already well represented |

## Recommendation

Specify and visually review one bounded normal-matrix proof-memory exemplar.
Do not start by building a scheduler or matrix library. First prove that one
searchable proof, one minimal semantic matrix scene, direct state links, and a
small prompt set form a coherent memory object.

This candidate has the best balance because:

- the proof is short but not trivial;
- the learner must preserve several symbol roles and dependencies;
- the decisive inference has a clear visual-attention target;
- prediction and boundary-variation prompts are intrinsic to the proof; and
- the current Article, native-KaTeX, semantic-address, deterministic-seek, and
  flashcard-projection foundations should carry most of the implementation.

## First Slice

Produce a human-reviewable exemplar specification containing:

1. the original proof outline and prerequisites;
2. the symbol-and-role ledger;
3. approximately five semantic checkpoints;
4. the single matrix-attention sequence that must outperform static media;
5. eight to twelve draft prompt intents, not polished copied cards;
6. static, direct-link, and delayed-return acceptance scenarios;
7. a route and bundle budget; and
8. a reversible implementation boundary.

Stop for review before implementing a generic matrix motif, scheduling system,
or second proof.

## Why Not The Fastest Existing Lesson?

Fraction composition can cheaply prove the projection mechanics, but those
mechanics are already present. The product uncertainty is whether KP can lower
working-memory load and improve durable reconstruction for a sophisticated
argument. The normal-matrix proof tests that question without taking on SVD or
Gödel-scale scope.
