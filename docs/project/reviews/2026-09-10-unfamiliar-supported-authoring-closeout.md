## Limits and hindsight

One author worked sequentially and learned across cases. Required repository
context included extra roadmap summaries; there was no implementation inspection
or inherited chat, but this was not an access-control isolation experiment.
Timing started inconsistently and omitted portions of author work. Do not claim
total authoring time, speedup, token cost, general LLM success rate or learner
comprehension. Browser WebKit is not a physical Safari gesture test. Five small
same-family cases do not establish arbitrary LaTeX-chain support.

The trial was worth doing: it tested whether the compositor work could stop
growing while an unfamiliar author made working content. Here it could. The
remaining friction was support-message interpretation, context overhead and
editorial intent—not a demonstrated need for another general rendering layer.
The packet now explains the observed unsupported case and repairs stale guidance;
that informed correction is not a second cold-start result.

Twenty slices provided a useful first audit but were expensive relative to five
small sources. Do not repeat this ceremony for routine variants. Reuse the
retained runner and scope-specific checks; reserve broad gates for real promotion
or release boundaries. No new framework should be inferred from this closeout.
