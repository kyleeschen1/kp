## Recommendation

After the stabilization slice, build one independently reversible integration
exemplar and stop for human review:

\[
\int x^2\,dx
\longrightarrow
\frac{x^{2+1}}{2+1}+C
\longrightarrow
\frac{x^3}{3}+C.
\]

This example is preferable to jumping directly from `integral x^2 dx` to the
answer because it exposes the applied rule and lets the two `2 + 1`
evaluations use governed contributor-fusion authority rather than unexplained
replacement. It is still bounded: no arbitrary symbolic exponent, definite
bounds, substitution, area interpretation, or Fundamental Theorem claim.

Integration supplies genuinely new semantics:

- an integral operator owns a named integrand scope;
- the differential `dx` identifies the integration variable rather than
  behaving as decorative suffix paint;
- the source exponent contributes to two introduced `n + 1` roles without
  claiming that one paint occurrence exists twice;
- the constant of integration is necessary introduced mathematical structure;
  and
- operator discharge remains distinct from evaluation and native settlement.

It also provides the right pressure for the current derivative scope-box note.
Before either caller shares presentation machinery, represent scope visibility
as renderer policy—`outline`, `salience-only`, or `none`—while preserving the
semantic operator-scope identity. Keep the derivative's approved appearance
unchanged and let human review determine the integration treatment. Do not
promote the box itself as operator syntax.

## Alternative: Inequality Reversal

If the goal is maximum visual and semantic distance from differentiation,
choose a bounded inequality exemplar instead:

\[
-x < 3 \longrightarrow x > -3.
\]

The causal event is not merely moving a minus sign: multiplying both sides by
`-1` reverses the order relation. A good implementation would preserve the
two sides, make the negative operation legible, and treat `< -> >` as a
verified relation-state change. This is a strong pedagogical animation and a
good governed-generation test, but it does not help settle the operator-scope
presentation policy.

## Other Options

- **Trigonometric rewrite:** begin with a syntax-sensitive equivalence such as
  `1/sin(x) -> csc(x)`. This is cheap and valuable for inverse-versus-
  reciprocal normalization, but likely reuses existing replacement and
  wrapping motifs rather than discovering much new choreography.
- **Limit simplification:** use a removable-discontinuity example only after
  the domain restriction and one-sided/at-point distinction are explicit.
  Otherwise attractive cancellation could teach a false equality at the
  excluded input.
- **Graph2D translation:** animate `f(x) -> f(x-h)` with a linked curve shift.
  This strongly demonstrates cross-view identity, but should be its own
  equation/graph checkpoint rather than part of the KaTeX benchmark slice.
- **Graph3D:** retain the accepted bounded rotation/projection proof. It is the
  largest semantic and lifecycle pressure case and should not share an
  implementation loop with benchmark repair or a new symbolic exemplar.

## Execution Boundary

The recommended sequence is:

1. repair the two known benchmark drifts and restore a clean focused baseline;
2. add the integration operation, verified trace, roles, lineage, and typed
   unsupported cases;
3. build only the concrete exemplar above through the canonical equation host
   and Native KaTeX renderer;
4. produce the smallest contact sheet and lifecycle evidence needed for visual
   review; and
5. stop before a second integration caller, family promotion, broad
   authoring/generation closure, or benchmark-corpus expansion.

The preservation boundary is the approved derivative behavior, existing
mathematical authorities, compiler-owned motif selection, deterministic clock,
Native KaTeX endpoints, and typed-gap behavior. The rollback units are the
objective stabilization repair and the integration exemplar; they should not
be coupled into one irreversible tranche.
