# Algebra: count the whole, then count the parts

Authorized by the user's request to continue with algebra or linear algebra,
explicitly leaving the disliked calculus example alone. One reversible exemplar,
not a new long infrastructure portfolio. Theseus owns execution and review status.

The reader knows addition and multiplication but may read parentheses as mere
punctuation. Question: how can the same expression be counted as whole groups
and as individual contributions? Show `3(x+y)+2(x+y) → (3+2)(x+y) → 5(x+y) →
5x+5y`. A group can be treated as one quantity without knowing either member.
The equality preserves value; no values are assigned to x and y. Copies here
mean integer multiples of a real quantity, not a positive-area assumption.

Static baseline: the four equations with a sentence explaining each move.
Motion's proposed gain: track the intact repeated group during collection, then
track both contributions during distribution. Scrubbing should let the reader
inspect and reverse those changes. This is a perceptual hypothesis, not evidence
of learning. Transfer question: why cannot `3(x+y)+2(x+z)` be collected as five
copies of one unchanged group? Because the repeated expressions differ; another
valid rearrangement is possible, but this particular collection is not licensed.
Keep that contrast in prose rather than inventing an unsupported animation.
