# Document theater reading

Accepted direction: 2026-11-09. These are editorial and presentation defaults,
not evidence of learner comprehension or a universal renderer contract.

## Orient the first encounter

A guided theater begins with a meaningful annotation already accompanying its
first native frame. Identify what the learner should inspect and why it matters;
do not require an extra scroll merely to discover the point of the display.
This is an ordinary authored opening beat, resolved by shared attention from the
semantic reading position, not an extra timer or an invented renderer fallback.
An explicit prediction or unaided inspection question can justify withholding
the answer. A saved destination restores its own attention, not a forced intro.

Annotations receive their own reading moment and yield to normal prose. Keep
their prose font and anchored alignment. Several-target notes remain a separate
design question; this rule does not invent a new anchor topology.

## Keep beats small without weakening the explanation

Give a theater beat one instructional job: one observation, prediction, action
or connection. Usually one or two short sentences are enough. Roughly 20–40
words is a useful editing prompt, not a minimum, maximum or compiler gate.
Read the beat at its actual pace and size; conceptual load matters more than
word count. Avoid multiplying beats just to satisfy a quota.

Keep full arguments, examples, qualifications and transfer questions in ordinary
prose, placed where their reasoning is needed. Do not silently truncate them,
shrink type, hide prerequisites in optional branches or replace reasons with
names. The explanation-first worksheet still precedes translation into beats.

## Paragraph rhythm

Use a consistent modest first-line indent for explanatory paragraphs, including
theater prose. The document reader owns the shared style (currently 1.25em), not
individual articles. Headings, bylines, controls and anchored labels keep their
own alignment; an annotation is not an indented body paragraph merely because
it uses the prose font. Headings remain modest under the accepted reader style.

## Exemplar roles

A technical exemplar proves authoring, rendering or lifecycle behavior. A
teaching exemplar must also address a consequential learner question, make the
reasoning follow, and support a changed-case check. A featured exemplar needs
editorial judgment beyond “the system can render this.” Keep those claims separate.

What counts remains a technical reference. Its count-versus-quantity reasoning
was judged clear; its contrived topic should not define featured quality. The
existing fractional-equation reader is the next teaching source to develop,
around choosing moves that preserve solutions. This is selection for development,
not a declaration that its current explanation is exceptional.

## Bounded adoption

The first candidate is the product reading at `/reader/what-counts/?reading=product`.
Its opening uses an existing checked state and existing notes; the reader owns
early presence. Shared document paragraph indentation is approved. Broader
attention rollout waits for this candidate's review and a different caller.
Old manuscript bytes and addresses remain pinned. No schema, universal note
requirement or catalogue migration follows merely from recording these defaults.
