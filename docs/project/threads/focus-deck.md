## Goal

Establish the Focus Deck as KP's first compact, portable Kinetic Figure format:
a document-backed sequence of semantic attentional beats that can be viewed,
linked, embedded, combined, and later projected into practice without
duplicating semantic or animation authority.

The working product image is “interoperable, executable GIFs,” with the
important differences that the learner can pause at meaningful states, inspect
semantic objects, follow direct links, search the source, control motion, and
compose figures safely.

## Current Decision

The Focus Deck is a projection, not a source format. It should derive from the
same Article blocks, vignette references, semantic focus, and motion blocks as
ordinary reading. The existing `kp.article-deck.v1` derivation is the leading
seam, but it is not automatically the finished product contract.

The approved log-product and delta-epsilon Kinetic Figures remain the shell and
hermeneutic evidence. Their paragraph, local-split, glance-coupled, stacked,
and Context/Inspect variants remain preserved rather than replaced. The
supply-tax caller now tests the more consequential question: whether one deck
can make a graph shift, two prices, a quantity contraction, redistribution,
government revenue, and deadweight loss inspectable without turning into a
continuous animation or a miniature slide deck.

Flashcards remain a sibling projection. Existing `KpFlashcardSpec` semantics,
selector masks, prediction/explanation kinds, and validation remain preserved;
the current slice adds no scheduler or card UI.

See
`../decisions/2026-08-29-focus-deck-initial-portable-figure-priority.md`.

## Product Laws Under Test

- One beat communicates one coherent attentional act, not one video frame.
- Semantic navigation and motion playback are separate control systems.
- A beat may be still; a transition may occur between two beats.
- The Article remains the searchable source of truth and the deck preserves an
  exact return path to it.
- Direct URLs restore semantic state directly rather than replaying history.
- Inactive figures consume no animation loop.
- The compact frame is intrinsic and embeddable, not fixed to presentation
  software geometry.
- Unsupported automatic derivation returns an editorial repair gap rather than
  inventing slide boundaries or generic animation.

## Active Exemplar

The canonical asset is
`animation.algebra.log-product.equivalence-frame`, presenting
`ln(xy) = ln(x) + ln(y)` through four beats:

1. orient;
2. locate the product;
3. inspect the product law;
4. apply and settle.

The rule-to-result edge owns the canonical animation. The source remains as a
witness while the equivalent right side is constructed. The rule is
contextual support rather than permanent stage content. The deck introduces
no new animation clock, semantic store, or KaTeX ownership path.

The current human-checkpoint shell uses a persistent left Steps column on
wide screens and a compact step disclosure on narrow screens. Stage, prose,
playback, and prominent Previous/Next controls share one main column. The
product remains the attentional target from recognition through rule matching;
only entry into `apply-product-law` transfers attention and starts motion.

The second bounded caller asks why
`lim(x -> 1) (x^2 - 1)/(x - 1) = 2` despite the removable hole. Its five beats follow
`establish whole -> isolate hole -> set epsilon challenge -> relate delta input
window -> reintegrate proof`. It uses the shared reader timeline clock and a
pure local graph projection; it does not claim support from the governed
quadratic-translation Graph2D frontend. A Context/Inspect lens changes only
resolved salience, never beat identity, presence, geometry, or playhead. Both
callers use the same symbol-only semantic navigation and playback vocabulary,
while their deck layouts remain separate until review reveals the true shared
boundary.

The active third caller starts with exact linear curves
`P_D = 12 - Q` and `P_S = 2 + Q`, then applies a per-unit tax of `4`. Its
untaxed equilibrium is `(Q, P) = (5, 7)`; its taxed outcome is
`Q = 3`, consumer price `9`, and producer price `5`. Exact welfare truth stays
in the economics model: consumer and producer surplus each fall from `25/2`
to `9/2`, government revenue is `12`, and deadweight loss is `4`. The SVG
projects that truth; it never infers it from painted geometry. Eight semantic
beats separate orientation, tax input, supply translation, the price wedge,
quantity contraction, surplus redistribution, revenue, and deadweight loss.
Adjacent navigation may animate the edge, while distant or rapid navigation
must resolve directly to a deterministic endpoint using the existing reader
clock.

## Resolved Firefox Exponent Role Transfer

The compact Focus Deck's `logged.exponent -> extracted.coefficient` transition
now runs through the canonical compositor in Firefox. The failure was a
measurement artifact rather than a semantic or choreography defect.

All three engines reported an exact `0.7` computed font-size ratio, matching
DOM text-width ratios within subpixel tolerance and with no wrapper transforms.
Firefox alone returned size-hinted Canvas ink ratios of approximately `0.7173`
horizontal and `0.6935` vertical, which incorrectly crossed the existing
`1.025` anisotropy gate.

The shared typography handoff now uses the computed font ratio only when the
same paint fingerprint, font revision, metric style fingerprint, and DOM text
box independently prove a uniform typographic role change. Non-equivalent or
geometrically inconsistent glyphs still fail closed. The global anisotropy
threshold is unchanged, and the repair contains no browser, glyph, equation,
or caller-specific offset.

The stable multi-card browser command now includes Firefox. Its 28 Chromium
and Firefox checks pass across automatic playback, material ownership, rapid
navigation, readiness queuing, static fallback, remount, reverse, slider,
direct-link, desktop, and phone behavior. The focused WebKit role-transfer
representative and Firefox log-product and log-quotient preservation checks
also pass. This is repair evidence for one shared measurement mechanism, not a
new animation-family promotion.

## Resolved Safari Cadence And Compact KaTeX Fit

Arrow traversal no longer exposes one specially slowed exponent-role-transfer
edge. Browser measurement showed the prior card policy mixing immediate
attention-only steps, roughly two-second ordinary rewrites, and a four-second
role transfer in both Chromium and WebKit. All motion-owning `act` phases now
use the same card-local `0.85` automatic tempo; the canonical operation windows
and semantic endpoints remain unchanged.

The equation card also retains the last programmatic passage offset until a
matching delayed scroll event has been consumed. Safari can deliver that event
after snap restoration; it must not reclaim the deterministic playhead and
turn a settled arrow action into a native scrub. Real passage displacement
still enters the existing native-scroll path.

Compact-host KaTeX now uses explicit endpoint and material-layer selectors, a
Safari text-size lock, and a container-relative `1.8rem` to `2.8rem` scale with
a viewport fallback. This is a bounded Focus Deck fit policy, not a change to
KaTeX, native endpoint geometry, or the shared compositor. The executable
browser check samples endpoint and in-flight material paint across every
rewrite at phone width and verifies the compact desktop ceiling.

The stable multi-card browser command now runs Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit.
All 48 checks pass across readiness, automatic and rapid navigation, forward
and reverse material continuity, delayed-scroll ownership, direct restoration,
compact paint bounds, desktop, and phone behavior. Human review remains the
gate for the adjusted equation scale and cadence.

## Stop Conditions

- A deck requires separately authored semantic or animation truth.
- Native find or accessibility requires duplicate hidden prose.
- Direct restoration depends on replaying prior scenes.
- The host introduces a deck-local animation clock or renderer fork.
- The first exemplar pressures a shared schema before human visual approval.
