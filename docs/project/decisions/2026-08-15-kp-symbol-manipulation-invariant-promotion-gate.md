# Decision 2026-08-15: Symbol-Manipulation Invariants And Promotion Gate

Date: 2026-08-15
Status: accepted

## Decision

Treat symbolic animation as an animated proof of a semantic tree rewrite.
Semantic correspondence, identity, provenance, ownership, grouping, and causal
structure govern motion before renderer path planning or paint.

The corrected native-KaTeX `2^x = 7` log/exponent animation remains the
canonical discovery exemplar. Its emerging rules are accepted as candidate
symbol-manipulation invariants, but they are not catalogue-wide policy yet.
Promotion requires three contrasting operations:

1. log quotient, to pressure atomic subtrees and fraction-shell causality;
2. distribution, to pressure explicit fan-out and operator attachment;
3. cancellation, to pressure justified removal and a visible cancellation
   witness.

Each caller must pass a human visual checkpoint before cross-caller promotion.
Only the smallest boundary evidenced by all three may become shared typed
infrastructure.

## Candidate Invariants

### Semantic material

- Every visible semantic entity has total correspondence coverage.
- Surviving material is classified as identity-preserving or role-changing;
  it never disappears and respawns.
- Introduced, removed, cancelled, merged, and split material has explicit
  provenance. Visual glyph equality never establishes identity.
- At every sampled instant, a continuant has one visual owner: source,
  transit, or target.
- Direct seek, rewind, interruption, and replay sample the same deterministic
  semantic transformation.

### Tree structure and locality

- An unchanged semantic subtree moves atomically unless the authored operation
  explicitly opens, extracts, distributes, splits, merges, or cancels it.
- Operators, signs, separators, and punctuation remain attached to their
  owning expression; they do not receive unrelated trajectories.
- Motion is local to an explicit rewrite frontier. Unaffected context remains
  anchored or reflows as one coherent context unit.
- A semantic role change may interpolate measured endpoint metrics. It must
  not scale an unrelated expression or replace native endpoint typography.
- Motion units distinguish exact rigidity, similarity-preserving motion, and
  order-preserving reflow rather than treating every group identically.

### Causal structure

- Structural shells such as parentheses, fraction bars, radicals, and brackets
  respond to semantic material rather than acting as independent decorations.
- Causality is expressed through semantic events such as clearance established,
  containment crossed, required material arrived, target recognizable, and
  native endpoint ready. Authors do not own normalized timing constants.
- Balanced applications share one semantic branch schedule.
- Fan-in and fan-out targets become recognizable only after their required
  source material is ready.
- Cancellation removes material only through an explicit cancellation relation
  and perceptible witness.

### Candidate homomorphic-fusion grammar

The quotient exemplar introduces one provisional operation shape:

```text
H(a) o H(b) -> H(a star b)
```

This covers logarithm laws directly and may later describe homomorphisms,
linear maps, and differential operators. Its current governing principles are:

- Repeated source operator applications form a many-to-one successor cohort.
  No source occurrence is selected as the arbitrary survivor.
- Operator-glyph fusion and application-level fusion are separate semantic
  relations. A renderer may show restrained glyph convergence while the
  application successor becomes recognizable only after its argument and
  structural requirements settle.
- Arguments preserve identity while changing grammatical roles. In the
  quotient exemplar, the left and right arguments arc into numerator and
  denominator positions; that path choice is presentation, not algebra.
- The source connector licenses construction of the target inner operation,
  but it never becomes target structure. In particular, subtraction does not
  become the fraction bar.
- Source enclosures retire after their material departs. Target enclosures and
  constructors are new structure introduced after the target argument becomes
  recognizable.
- Rewind uses the same correspondence and choreography in reverse; it does not
  compile an unrelated visual trick.

The typed choreography is explicitly marked `candidate`. It may be reused by
pressure callers, but it is not a promoted catalogue-wide symbol-motion API
until the distribution and cancellation checkpoints complete the gate.

### Attention and rendering

- Material presence, visual ownership, and instructional salience are separate
  axes. Lifecycle opacity cannot be used as a routing shortcut, while later
  scene-level salience may still render persistent material as context.
- Native KaTeX owns settled typography and accessibility. Authored case and
  operator identity must survive host CSS.
- Collision repair may adjust geometry but may not split certified motion
  units, change provenance, or fade continuants.
- Every trajectory settles on measured target geometry before target ownership
  begins; no final-frame snap or premature fade is allowed.

## Typed Architecture Direction

Keep operation-independent laws separate from operation-specific motifs.
Candidate shared types are:

- continuant and role-metric rules;
- motion units with `exact`, `similarity`, or `ordered` cohesion;
- operator and punctuation attachment rules;
- explicit derivation and cancellation rules;
- structural-shell lifecycle rules;
- rewrite-frontier and context-anchor rules;
- semantic causal constraints compiled into an acyclic dependency graph.

The compiler should mint nominal authority only after validating total
coverage, unique ownership, disjoint motion-unit membership, legal provenance,
attachment closure, causal acyclicity, and rewind symmetry. The native renderer
should consume that authority after endpoint measurement and before path
planning, collision repair, sampling, and paint.

The existing `continuous-opaque` rule is provisional. A promoted contract
should express continuous semantic material presence independently from
scene-level salience styling.

## Reason

The corrected log/exponent animation improved when `ln`, `x`, parentheses, and
`ln(2)` stopped being treated as interchangeable glyph tracks:

- `ln` is a persistent semantic operator;
- `x` is a continuant changing grammatical role and measured metrics;
- parentheses are a structural shell;
- `ln(2)` is an unchanged semantic subtree during division.

These distinctions explain several earlier regression classes, including
premature fading, orphaned operators, broken chunks, inconsistent scaling,
and last-frame jumps. They are stronger than animation-specific trajectory
recipes, but one caller is insufficient evidence for universal types.

## Consequences

- The active log/exponent run continues through the quotient caller, then adds
  distribution and cancellation pressure before any shared promotion.
- The former linearity-only pressure step is superseded by the more diagnostic
  distribution and cancellation sequence. A later linear-map caller remains a
  possible consumer, not the promotion gate for symbolic manipulation.
- Shared invariant types may grow only when a caller demonstrates a missing
  semantic distinction. Exact paths, phase durations, palette values, and
  renderer-specific geometry remain presentation policy.
- Existing symbolic assets are not migrated automatically.
- Each operation remains an independently reversible rollback unit.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Promote from the corrected log exemplar immediately:** rejected because it
  would overfit wrapping and exponent extraction.
- **Keep all rules family-local:** rejected because ownership, provenance,
  subtree cohesion, and causal settlement already explain recurring defects
  across symbolic families.
- **Use the linearity equation as the only second pressure caller:** deferred;
  it exercises homomorphic shape but does not adequately test cancellation or
  operator attachment.
- **Encode fixed trajectories and timing windows:** rejected because those are
  renderer and choreography choices rather than semantic invariants.

## Follow-Ups

1. Complete the quotient contract, implementation, and human checkpoint.
2. Build one bounded distribution caller and stop for human review.
3. Build one bounded cancellation caller and stop for human review.
4. Compare all three callers, promote or reject each candidate invariant, and
   retain operation-specific motifs locally.
5. Only after promotion, propose a separately reviewable migration strategy
   for existing symbolic assets.
