## Direction

A question-oriented Focus Card resolves one useful uncertainty through an
inspectable explanation. The governing question supplies purpose, necessary
evidence, attention sequence, extraction boundaries and a stopping point; adding
a question mark to a topic title is insufficient.

Use a lightweight editorial brief: question; reader starting point; plain-language
answer; evidence to see/compare/manipulate; a changed-example or prediction check.
Ask at review: is the question worth answering, does the explanation answer it,
and can a reader use the connection on a changed example? These are pedagogical
acceptance questions, not machine-proven comprehension or efficacy claims.

One governing question is a coherence principle, not a fixed duration/beat limit.
Larger explanations can contain independently useful supporting questions. Preserve
referents, assumptions, necessary context and exact return when extracting them.
Keep transformations/correspondences in their domain-owned semantic authorities;
the editorial question neither supplies mathematical proof nor chooses geometry.

Question-oriented does not mean quiz-first: do not force guesses, hide answers
unnecessarily, manufacture suspense or require a permanently competing headline.
Still comparisons and pauses are valid beats. Focus Cards remain projections of
the shared explanation, not a new semantic source or a mandatory global schema.
