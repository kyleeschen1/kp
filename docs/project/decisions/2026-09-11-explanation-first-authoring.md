# Explanation first; crystallize what teaches

Status: accepted direction; gradient draft still needs pedagogical review.
Authority: user accepted the explanation-design recommendation with
“i agree. implement”.

## Decision

Develop the explanation around the learner's question and inferential needs
before fitting it into a Focus Card or selecting animation beats. Conversation,
prose, equations and sketches can all discover that explanation. This is not a
prose-only doctrine, a ban on visual discovery, or a requirement to understand a
concept without the very diagram that makes it comprehensible.

Preserve the connecting reasoning, motivation, examples and useful redundancy
when translating into an executable artifact. A cue locating evidence does not
replace an explanation of why the evidence supports the conclusion. When prose
does not fit, revisit decomposition or projection before removing necessary
reasoning. Compact retrieval units need not also teach every prerequisite on a
reader's first encounter.

Use the editorial worksheet at
`../authoring/explanation-first-worksheet.md` for new conceptual explanations and
repairs of an unclear “why”. Reuse an accepted explanatory baseline for ordinary
source-only variants. No universal runtime schema, compulsory extra approval for
every card, or claim of type-level pedagogical sufficiency is introduced.

Distinguish mathematical/semantic correctness, presentation quality, and learner
understanding. Lightweight prediction or explanation questions inform the last;
neither fluent prose nor passing renderer tests proves learning. Conversation
should expose a missing bridge rather than merely select another visual effect.
