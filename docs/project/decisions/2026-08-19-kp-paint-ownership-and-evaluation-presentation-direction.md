# Paint Ownership Continuity and Evaluation Presentation Families

Date: 2026-08-19
Status: accepted direction; implementation requires a reviewed plan

## Decision

Treat the visible jerk in the `1/3 + 1/6` pressure caller as a sequence-level
paint-ownership defect, not as a caller timing problem. After relocating the
Review panel, implement one structural Native KaTeX ownership repair before
continuing fraction choreography:

1. render and measure each shared semantic endpoint once;
2. give adjacent phases the same immutable endpoint handle;
3. preserve compound presentation groups and semantic leaf ownership in the
   same observation;
4. select either the compound carrier or its leaf carriers without clearing,
   rebinding, or remeasuring the endpoint;
5. require an equivalent-pose ownership certificate at every adjacent phase
   seam; and
6. verify continuity per semantic leaf rather than only through the union of
   the visible equation bounds.

The renderer-neutral law is exclusive continuous paint ownership with exact
native endpoints. Native KaTeX may realize that law through hierarchical
compound/leaf ownership; SVG, Canvas, WebGL, and cross-renderer handoffs may
need different adapters. Do not introduce one physical ownership mechanism
for every renderer.

Also replace the assumption that every operator evaluation should use one
universal shrink-collapse-expand treatment. Governed operation semantics stay
shared, while presentation selects one explicit evaluation family:

- **punctuated substitution**: hold, mark the evaluated subexpression, and
  replace it at a stable anchor;
- **result reception**: establish the result at its native destination while
  contributors hand material into it;
- **contributor fusion**: gather contributors into a contact region when
  combination itself is the teaching idea;
- **carrier-preserving simplification**: retain one semantically justified
  carrier for identity operations such as multiplication by one;
- **operator aperture**: let an operator visibly receive arguments and emit a
  result when the operator-as-process is instructional; and
- **expanded calculation**: expose a local intermediate calculation when a
  novice needs more than an inline rewrite.

Each family requires its own canonical exemplar and human checkpoint before
promotion. A caller or planner must select the family from instructional and
semantic intent; matching glyph text must not infer a carrier or animation.
Punctuated substitution is the conservative fallback candidate, not yet a
promoted default.

## Operational Model

Evaluation presentation is two related but separate compiler decisions:

1. the **evaluation family** states the instructional metaphor; and
2. the **paint handoff** states how visible ownership passes between the
   source and result without becoming semantic authority.

Authors and model planners may request an evaluation family from verified
operation semantics and instructional intent. They must not author geometry,
opacity, timing, renderer nodes, or a physical trajectory. A constrained
presentation registry maps an accepted family to a versioned recipe. That
recipe selects one compatible renderer-neutral handoff:

- **matched-pose substitution** for a stable-anchor replacement;
- **progressive replacement** for a result that receives attention over time;
- **persistent-carrier transfer** when semantic lineage proves one carrier
  survives;
- **contact occlusion** when contributor combination or operator reception is
  itself instructional; or
- **discrete cut** when continuity would falsely imply identity or material
  flow.

The mapping is not an unrestricted cross-product. Recipe validation must
reject carrier preservation without identity evidence, contributor fusion
without material contributors, and operator aperture without an explicit
operator-as-process teaching intent. Unsupported combinations remain typed
gaps; they do not silently fall back to generic fades or successor motion.

The selected recipe compiles into the existing paint-continuity and rendered-
scene machinery. A verified plan names semantic source, target, catalyst, and
carrier bundles; stable anchor and baseline policy; ownership topology;
stationary context; and exact native endpoints. It contains no caller-local
coordinates. Native KaTeX resolves the plan against immutable ink
measurements, while other renderers may implement the same continuity law
through their own adapters.

Every handoff must preserve these laws:

- no blank interval with no meaningful paint;
- no interval with two complete competing states;
- exactly one dominant owner for each semantic leaf;
- stable anchor, baseline, and unrelated context;
- no mandatory collapse of meaningful paint to a zero-size point;
- exact native start and end geometry; and
- identical state under playback, direct seek, reverse, interruption, and URL
  restoration.

Callers reference versioned recipe IDs rather than implementing choreography.
Tuning a compatible recipe updates all callers of that version; a breaking
change creates a new version and an explicit migration. The intended pipeline
is therefore:

```text
verified operation semantics
-> instructional evaluation family
-> constrained presentation recipe
-> verified paint-continuity plan
-> renderer-specific measured realization
-> deterministic paint
```

The first comparison must hold the mathematical endpoints constant and expose
matched-pose substitution, progressive replacement, and contributor fusion
for `2 * 3 -> 6`; `2 * 1 -> 2` separately pressures persistent-carrier
transfer. Human review selects any default before a second operator shape or
catalogue migration is attempted.

## Reason

The current pressure caller coalesces `2/2` into one structural paint owner for
introduction, then rebinds the same native endpoint into numerator, bar, and
denominator owners for the following fraction-equivalence phase. Independent
renderer sessions can therefore disagree at an internal boundary even when
the equation's aggregate bounds remain stable. Timing changes can hide that
seam but cannot remove the duplicated endpoint authority.

The evaluation problem has a different cause. Simultaneously visible source
and result glyphs make small symbolic scenes crowded, while literal scaling to
zero followed by scaling from zero looks mechanical and weakens the impression
of transformation. There is no single honest physical correspondence between
the glyphs in every operation and the glyphs in its result. Presentation must
therefore distinguish deliberate substitution, material combination,
identity preservation, and operator reception instead of forcing them through
one visual metaphor.

## Consequences

- The `1/3 + 1/6` caller remains unpromoted and stops at `2/6 + 1/6`.
- The unit-factor jerk must be repaired in shared sequence/renderer
  infrastructure rather than patched with dwell, opacity, or caller-local
  coordinates.
- Shared endpoint measurements become immutable for the lifetime of a
  renderer session and invalidate atomically on font or viewport revision.
- A phase sequence fails closed when adjacent endpoint handles or ownership
  inventories do not match.
- Direct seek, rewind, replay, interruption, and ordinary playback must sample
  the same seam state without replay-dependent reconstruction.
- Review evidence must compare semantic leaf geometry and exclusive ownership
  around seams; aggregate outer bounds alone are insufficient.
- Successor synthesis remains valid semantic and continuity authority. It no
  longer implies that every evaluation uses contributor-fusion choreography.
- Evaluation timing and geometry remain motif-owned and tunable after each
  exemplar checkpoint.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Tune the current segment weights or add endpoint dwell:** rejected because
  it masks a duplicated ownership boundary.
- **Cross-fade independently reconstructed endpoints:** rejected because it
  permits double ownership and preserves the internal geometric snap.
- **Keep one universal successor-synthesis animation:** rejected because the
  visual metaphor becomes noisy or semantically misleading across different
  operation kinds.
- **Use literal glyph-outline morphing:** deferred as a specialized experiment;
  arbitrary KaTeX glyph contours are brittle, expensive, and do not establish
  semantic lineage.
- **Build a universal cross-renderer paint graph:** rejected. The continuity
  law is shared, but each renderer retains its own physical realization.

## Follow-ups

1. Relocate the wide Catalogue Review composer from the cramped lower-left
   catalogue rail to a fully visible inspector-side drawer; retain a bounded
   narrow-screen sheet and keep Save reachable without covering playback.
2. Add shared Native KaTeX endpoint handles and hierarchical ownership views
   by extending the existing rendered-scene, presentation-group, paint-
   continuity, and compositor seams rather than creating a parallel renderer.
3. Repair the common-denominator unit-factor handoff and add per-entity seam,
   direct-seek, rewind, font-settlement, and resize-invalidation evidence.
4. Pressure the ownership repair with a structurally different existing
   compound caller, preferably structural wrapping or fraction split/merge,
   before family-level promotion.
5. Prototype separate evaluation-family exemplars only after ownership is
   stable. Compare punctuated substitution, result reception, and contributor
   fusion on the paired `2 * 1` and `2 * 3` evaluation without changing its
   mathematical endpoints.
6. Route each exemplar through the constrained, versioned presentation-recipe
   registry and the existing paint-continuity compiler. Keep callers free of
   timing, coordinates, opacity schedules, and renderer-node authority.
7. Use a second operator shape to decide which family, if any, becomes the
   conservative default. Keep carrier-preserving and operator-aperture motion
   opt-in unless their semantic preconditions are verified.
8. Revisit fraction-equivalence promotion and common-denominator addition only
   after the ownership and evaluation checkpoints pass.
