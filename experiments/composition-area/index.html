Why can the shapes differ but the areas agree?

A linear map scales every signed area by its determinant. Applying A then B multiplies the scale factors: det(BA) = det(B) det(A). The scalar factors commute, even when the matrices do not. Equal determinants therefore do not imply equal transformations.

A negative determinant reverses orientation. Geometric area is the absolute value of signed area. A zero determinant collapses the square to a line or point; a later linear map cannot restore the lost area.

The animation interpolates corresponding corners between the exact endpoint maps. It is not physical motion or a promise of invertibility in between. Use the three named steps to inspect the actual maps. During motion, area readouts describe the destination.
