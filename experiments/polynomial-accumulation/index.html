Both horizontal axes run from −3 to 3. Each graph keeps its vertical scale fixed while you sweep. The dashed line marks the start a; the solid line and dot mark x.

A thin strip contributes its height times its width, with a negative height below the axis. An antiderivative F has derivative f, so the accumulated total from a to x is F(x) − F(a). The running total starts at zero at a; its slope at x is f(x).

Reversing the bounds negates the signed integral. It leaves the same geometric region, so geometric area does not change. When moving left, above-axis contributions subtract and below-axis contributions add.

Inspect the exact totals

Bounds and the moving endpoint use hundredths. The power rule gives exact rational totals; plotted curves and displayed decimals are approximations.

Predict a cancellation

Choose f(t) = t, start at −2 and end at 2. Why is the signed total zero while the geometric area is 4?

The equal triangles contribute −2 and +2 to the integral, so they cancel. Their geometric areas add to 4. Reversing these bounds still gives zero; try unequal bounds to see a nonzero total change sign.
