Where do sine and cosine come from?

Turn a radius around a circle of radius 1. The point’s horizontal coordinate is cosine; its vertical coordinate is sine.

The point returns after each full turn. The wave marker wraps to 0 at that boundary; the entered angle keeps its turns.

Angles begin on the positive x-axis; positive angles turn counterclockwise. One radian measures an arc as long as the radius, so a full turn is 2π2\pi2π radians, or 360°. The radius remains 1: cos⁡2θ+sin⁡2θ=1\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1cos2θ+sin2θ=1.
